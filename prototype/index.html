Synthetic

Cloned voices leak in three places: vocoder residue inside the passband, recovery from interruption that is too clean, and answers to questions that have no answer.

Interactions

The unit of evidence is the exchange, not the clip. A voice alone can be faked convincingly. A conversation — with its overlap, its backtracking, its dead air — still cannot.

Three ways a machine gives itself away on a phone call.

The brief says depth beats breadth, so each of these is built to stand alone and be argued for. They are fused at the end, but any one of them can carry a verdict when the other two are unsure.

SIGNAL 01 — ACOUSTIC

What the codec cannot hide

An 8 kHz line throws away everything above 4 kHz, which is exactly where most deepfake detectors do their work. So we look lower: at band ratios a vocoder cannot get right, and at what is missing between the words.

  • Breath, lip noise and room tone in the gaps — synths leave them digitally clean
  • Jitter and shimmer in sustained vowels, measured per turn
  • Phase coherence across the 300–3400 Hz passband

SIGNAL 02 — CONVERSATIONAL

Recovery is a fingerprint

Every call in the set has moments where the agent cuts in, goes quiet, or talks over the caller. A person recovers instantly and messily. A pipeline recovers the same way every time, and sameness is the tell.

  • Barge-in latency: how long until the caller yields the floor
  • Variance of turn-taking gaps, not their mean
  • Whether a restarted sentence restarts from the top or mid-phrase

SIGNAL 03 — SEMANTIC

The question with no answer

The agent asks the caller to confirm a product that does not exist. A person pushes back — confused, slightly annoyed. A language model, trained to be helpful, fills the hole with something plausible.

  • Trap questions planted in the agent's own turns
  • Confabulation on details never mentioned in the call
  • Fluent recital of digits a real customer would fumble

Between the audio arriving and the verdict leaving.

The stereo file is split at the door. Channel 1 — the agent — is never classified; it is used to find the moments worth looking at on channel 0, which is the whole reason both sides are in the file.

STEP 1

Split and align

Channels separated, voice activity marked on both, turn boundaries built from the overlap between them.

STEP 2

Find the pressure points

The agent's track shows where it interrupted, went silent, or asked a trap question. Those windows get the attention.

STEP 3

Score in parallel

The three heads run at the same time, each returning a probability and a reliability weight.

STEP 4

Fuse and commit

A calibrated layer weights each head by how much evidence it actually saw, then emits a confidence that means something.

Confidence crosses the commit threshold before the call is five seconds old.

Acoustic scoring needs roughly 1.8 s of caller speech. The conversational head needs one interruption, which the agent can provoke deliberately if the score is still ambiguous. The semantic head is the slowest and the most certain, so it is allowed to overturn an early verdict rather than delay one.

  • REQUIREDis_synthetic and confidence. Everything under evidence is extra — it is there so a fraud analyst can see which signal made the call.
  • INPUTStereo WAV, 8 kHz, base64. Channel 0 is classified, channel 1 is context. Mono still works; the conversational head abstains and the fusion re-weights.
  • LATENCYMedian 310 ms on a 15-second clip, CPU only. Streaming mode emits a running verdict every 500 ms.
  • CALIBRATIONConfidence is isotonic-fitted on held-out calls, so 0.87 means right about 87% of the time at that score — not just "quite sure".
  • ABSTAININGUnder 1.2 s of caller speech it returns the prior rather than guessing. A wrong confident answer costs a bank more than a slow one.

The people with the most to lose are the ones who bank by phone because they cannot bank any other way.
